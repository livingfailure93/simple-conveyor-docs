Simple Conveyor

Conveyor belt puzzles for Unity that are generated, solved exactly and rated.

Simple Conveyor builds a conveyor belt puzzle from your settings, then proves it can be solved and works out its par, the fewest moves it takes. The player pushes the piece off a stop tile, and the belts carry it their way until it comes to rest on another one, so the puzzle is reading where each ride ends. It can add ice, switches that turn belts, gems to collect and holes, and it can keep resting places off the best route for chests or keys. Every puzzle is rated 1 to 5 stars and can be saved as a ScriptableObject, exported as a PNG or SVG picture, or played in the included 3D demo.

What it is

Simple Conveyor is a conveyor belt puzzle generator with a solver built in. A piece rests on a stop tile, and the player sends it north, east, south or west. It moves onto the next cell, and from there every belt it enters carries it on the way that belt points, turning it where the belts turn, until it reaches a stop tile and rests. You choose the board and what goes on it, and it builds a puzzle for you. Then it searches every position the puzzle can reach, so you know for certain that it can be solved and how many moves the best solution takes.

Every generated board keeps one hard rule: no belt ever points into a wall, a hole or off the board, in any switch state. A belt always carries the piece onto another belt, ice or a stop tile, so the player never watches a ride end against a wall for no reason, and no ride goes round for ever.

Everything is deterministic. The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle, and every puzzle keeps the settings and seed it was made from. When the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it says so in the puzzle's notes instead of quietly handing you something else. In the editor pictures and in the demo every kind of tile has its own shape as well as its own colour, so colour-blind players can tell them apart.

The rules

Belts carry the piece

A belt carries the piece one cell its way, onto the next belt or tile, so a ride follows the belts until it ends. A move into a wall or the board's edge right next to the piece doesn't happen.

Stop tiles stop it

The piece rests on the first stop tile a ride reaches. Stop tiles are never side by side, so every move rides at least one belt (or ice). The start and the goal are stop tiles too.

Ice keeps it going

With Ice on, ice keeps the piece going the way it was moving until a stop tile stops it, a belt turns it, or a wall or the edge leaves it resting on the ice in front.

Reach the goal

Rest on the goal to win. With gems on the board, the goal only counts once the piece has taken every one, by riding across it or stopping on it.

Switches turn belts

Each time the piece comes to rest on a switch, every switched belt turns: Reverse makes them run the other way, Rotate turns them a quarter turn clockwise.

Avoid the holes

On ice boards, a ride that ends in a hole loses the attempt. What that means is up to your game: the demo plays the fall and puts the piece back where it was.

A move the rules don't allow simply doesn't happen, and the rules say why. The solver and your game use the same rules, so they always agree.

Features

Proven par

The solver searches every position the puzzle can reach, so the par is the true minimum and not an estimate. It also counts how many best solutions there are.

Difficulty in stars

A 1 to 5 star rating from the par and the number of places the piece can come to rest, which is the room a player has to get lost in. Ask for a star count and the generator keeps trying until it gets one.

Board size

Boards from 4 to 32 cells each way, with stop tiles on up to 15 to 50% of the board, walls on up to 15%, and ice patches if you want them.

The belt rule

No belt points into a wall, a hole or off the board, in any switch state, and no ride goes round for ever. Nothing the piece can reach is a dead end.

Gimmicks

Up to 2 switches that reverse or rotate belts, 3 gems to collect, and 6 holes on ice boards.

Every gimmick matters

Each switch and gem is needed by the best solution. Make a switch a plain stop tile and the puzzle gets longer or can't be solved, and lift a gem and it gets shorter. Each hole is one that some ride of two or more cells really ends in.

Chest spots

Ask for up to 40 resting places off every best route, where a game can put a chest, a key or anything a detour should pay for. Each one comes with the extra moves going by it costs.

Printable export

Save any puzzle as a PNG or SVG: the colour view, a black on white printable sheet that draws every tile by its shape alone, or an answer key with the best solution drawn on.

ScriptableObjects

Save single puzzles or ordered packs, with inspectors that preview, re-check and regenerate them.

Play rules included

ConveyorRules tells your game what a move does and every cell it passes through, using the same rules the solver counted with, so the game and the par always agree. It also gives hints from any position.

Editor tooling

One Conveyor Generator window with 21 presets, an Auto button that fixes settings that fight each other, and generation on a background thread with a progress bar, Cancel and a time limit. Step through the best solution move by move on the board.

A 3D demo

Four game modes with endless puzzles and 42 ready-made levels, running belts, sounds and effects, all built in code. It works in URP, HDRP and the Built-in pipeline.

How it works

1

Configure

Pick the board, the gimmicks, the chest spots and the difficulty, in the Conveyor Generator window or with a ConveyorSettings object in code.

2

Lay the belts

Stop tiles go down first, never side by side. Every other cell becomes a belt, grown outward from a stop tile, so following the belts always ends on a stop tile.

3

Pick the start and goal

They go on the pair of stop tiles furthest apart in moves, where nothing the piece can reach is a dead end.

4

Climb

One change at a time: a belt points another way, a belt becomes a stop tile or back, a wall comes or goes, and (with Ice) a belt becomes ice or back. The change is kept when the puzzle doesn't get worse. A change that would break the belt rule or leave a dead end is never kept. With a star target, the climb stops short of the next star level.

5

Add the gimmicks

Switches, holes and gems each get a second climb on the finished board. Switches and gems go where the best solution needs them, and holes where a ride of two or more cells ends in them.

6

Rate and check

The finished puzzle is solved again from its start. It records the par, one best solution, how many best solutions exist, the chest spots, and the stars. A puzzle that misses what you asked for is built again from the next seed.

7

Use it

Save it as an asset, export a picture of it, play it in the demo, or read the ConveyorData in your own scripts.

Why "verified"

Belt puzzles are easy to get wrong by hand. A belt can point into a wall, two belts can pass the piece back and forth for ever, a shortcut can skip the whole route you designed, and one move can strand the piece for good. Simple Conveyor proves the things you design around:

  • The par is real. It is the shortest solution that exists, so a 20-move puzzle really takes 20 moves.
  • Every position is searched. A puzzle is only accepted when the search covered every position it can reach, in every switch state and with every set of gems taken.
  • No dead ends. Nothing the piece can reach is a place it can never win from.
  • The belt rule holds. No belt points into a wall, a hole or off the board, whichever way the switches have turned it.
  • Gimmicks earn their place. A switch or gem the best solution could do without isn't left on the board, and every hole is one that a ride of two or more cells really ends in.
  • Misses are reported. When a star count, a par window or a gimmick that doesn't fit can't be met, the puzzle's notes say what happened and what you got instead.

Two honest limits: stars follow the board's size (up to 6 x 6 gives 1 star, 7 x 7 2, 8 x 8 3, 10 x 10 4, and 12 x 12 and up 5), so a target far from the size comes back as the closest with a note. And gems lengthen puzzles fast: 3 gems take a 10 x 10 board from a par of about 26 to about 69, so cap gem puzzles with a maximum par or a star target.

Getting started

Open Window > Living Failure > Simple Conveyor > Conveyor Generator, pick a preset, press Generate and look at the analysis on the right. Then open the demo scene and play. The Quick Start walks through the whole loop in about five minutes.
