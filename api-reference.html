Assemblies and numbers

AssemblyWhat is in it
SimpleConveyorThe core: the generator, the solver, the play rules, the puzzle data, the pictures and the ScriptableObjects. It has no package dependencies, and everything but the ScriptableObjects is plain C# that also runs outside Unity.
SimpleConveyor.DemoThe 3D demo: the board, the game, the HUD, the hub, sounds and effects. It uses the Input System and Unity UI packages, and HDRP when that package is installed.
SimpleConveyor.EditorThe Conveyor Generator window, the inspectors, the documentation menu and the build step that keeps the demo's shader. Editor only.

Everything below is in the SimpleConveyor namespace unless it says SimpleConveyor.Demo. The core uses plain numbers throughout, and names cells by what they do (see Names in the data: a belt is an Arrow, a stop tile is Normal floor, a gem is a pickup and a hole is a Hazard):

  • Cells are numbered y * Width + x, with row 0 at the bottom, so cell 0 is the bottom left corner.
  • Directions are 0 North (y + 1), 1 East (x + 1), 2 South and 3 West. Dirs has them as North, East, South, West and Count, with Dx(dir), Dy(dir), Opposite(dir) and Name(dir).
  • Gems are tracked as a bit mask: bit i of a position's Taken is Pickups[i]. The switch state is a small number: how many times the switches have acted, wrapped (0 to 1 with Reverse, 0 to 3 with Rotate).

ConveyorGenerator

The entry point. Every method copies and clamps the settings first, so your object is never changed, and the same settings with the same seed always give the same puzzle. A null settings object means the defaults, and an empty seed means a fresh random one.

MethodWhat it does
ConveyorData Generate(ConveyorSettings settings)One puzzle from this exact seed, solved and rated.
ConveyorData GenerateVerified(ConveyorSettings settings, CancellationToken cancel = default) Tries the seed, then the seed with #1, #2 and so on, up to MaxAttempts, until a puzzle is sound (see IsSound) and rated TargetDifficulty stars (0 means any). On a miss it returns the closest puzzle with a note. If it is cancelled before the first puzzle, it throws OperationCanceledException.
ConveyorData GenerateVerified(ConveyorSettings settings, CancellationToken cancel, IProgress<ConveyorProgress> progress, double timeLimitSeconds = 0) The same with progress and a time limit. The progress hears which board it is on and what that board is doing. A cancel stops it within a fraction of a second, even in the middle of a board. Past the time limit (0 means none) it returns the closest puzzle of the boards that finished, with a note. When no board finished in time it throws TimeoutException, saying what the first board was still doing.
List<ConveyorData> GenerateBatch(ConveyorSettings settings, int count, CancellationToken cancel = default) A pack of verified puzzles with seeds root-0, root-1 and so on.
Task<ConveyorData> GenerateAsync(...), GenerateVerifiedAsync(...)The same on a background thread.
Task<List<ConveyorData>> GenerateBatchAsync(ConveyorSettings settings, int count, IProgress<int> progress = null, CancellationToken cancel = default) A batch on a background thread, reporting how many are done (1 to count).
bool IsSound(ConveyorData m, ConveyorSettings s)The hard requirements verified generation uses: proven by a complete search, solvable, no dead ends, the par inside the window, at least the chest spots asked for, no belt pointing into a wall, a hole or off the board in any switch state, at least the switches and gems asked for with every one needed by the best solution, and (with Ice) at least the live holes asked for.
bool HitsTargets(ConveyorData m, ConveyorSettings s)True when the puzzle has the stars the settings aim for (a target of 0 is always met).

ConveyorProgress holds Attempt and Attempts (the board being tried, from 1, and how many may be tried), Stage in plain words, Seconds since the puzzle started and Limit. Its ToString() reads like "board 2 of 40: placing gems (1.3 s)".

ConveyorSettings and enums

ConveyorSettings holds every input to the generator. Each field, its range and what it does is on the Generating page. It also has:

MemberWhat it does
ConveyorSettings Clamp()Puts every field into its valid range, in place, and returns the same object.
ConveyorSettings Clone()A copy.
MinSide, MaxSwitches, MaxPickups, MaxHolesThe smallest board side the generator makes, and the most of each it places: 4, 2, 3 and 6.
EnumValues
ConveyorSwitchModeWhat a switch does: Reverse (the switched belts run the other way, 2 states) or Rotate (a quarter turn clockwise, 4 states)
ConveyorBlockWhy a move can't start: None (it can), Edge (the board's edge is right next to the piece), Obstacle (a wall is right next to it) and Loop (belts would carry it round for ever, which a generated board never has)
ConveyorEndHow a move ends: Rest or Lost (it entered a hole)
ExportStyleAsShown, Printable and AnswerKey

ConveyorData

One puzzle, plain and serializable. Every cell is ice unless a list says otherwise, and a cell is in at most one of the board lists. The generator fills in the board and the verdict, and you can build one by hand too (see A puzzle by hand).

MemberWhat it is
int Width, HeightThe board's size in cells (2 to 32 a side).
List<ConveyorArrow> ArrowsThe belts. Each has a Cell and the Dir it runs (in switch state 0), and carries the piece one cell that way when it enters.
List<int> NormalThe stop tiles: the piece rests on the first one it enters.
List<int> ObstaclesThe walls: the piece can't enter them and rests in front of them.
List<int> HazardsThe holes: entering one ends the attempt.
List<int> PickupsThe gems: taken when a ride enters their cell. With any, the goal only wins once every one is taken.
List<int> Switches, List<int> SwitchedBelts, ConveyorSwitchMode SwitchModeThe switches (stop tiles, so also in Normal), the belts they turn, and how. Every switch turns the same belts.
int Start, int Goal, bool GoalCatchesWhere the piece starts, where it must come to rest to win, and whether passing over the goal stops it there (the goal is a stop tile on generated boards, so it always does).
string Seed, ConveyorSettings SettingsWhat it was made from, so it can be made again. Both are empty on a hand-made puzzle.
bool Complete, bool Solvable, int ParWhether the search finished (false means the budget cut it short, and nothing below is proven), whether it can be won, and the proven fewest moves (-1 when unsolvable or not proven).
long OptimalCount, bool CountComplete, bool UniqueHow many best solutions there are, whether that count is complete (it stops at 1,000,000,000), and whether there is exactly one.
bool CanGetStuck, float DeadEndShare, List<int> DeadEndStops Whether the piece can come to rest somewhere it can never win from, the share of resting places that are like that, and which cells they are. Generated puzzles have none.
long States, long PlacesThe positions the search explored, and the resting places the piece can reach, each counted once per switch state. The stars use Places.
List<int> SolutionOne best solution, as directions.
int Choices, Decoys, WrongTurns, LongDetours, FatalStops, FatalMovesHow the solution feels: stops with a real choice, stops where a wrong move ends nearer the goal, wrong moves from the solution's stops, wrong turns costing 4 or more extra moves, stops where a careless move ends in a hole, and moves from any resting place that end in a hole. Each is -1 when not proven.
List<int> SideStops, List<int> SideStopCostsThe chest spots: resting places on no best solution that can still reach the goal, the costliest first, and the extra moves going by each one costs.
int Difficulty, float ScoreThe 1 to 5 star rating (0 when not proven) and the score it comes from.
List<string> NotesPlain-words notes from the generator and the solver. The Generating page lists them.
Cells, X(cell), Y(cell), Cell(x, y), Clone()Cell arithmetic, and a deep copy.

The data comes from the rules engine Simple Conveyor shares with Simple Ice Slide, so it also has fields a generated conveyor puzzle leaves empty: Outside (cells cut off a shaped board), Cracked and CrackedBecomes (tiles that break), and Portals. The rules and the solver play them correctly on a hand-made board, and the pictures draw them, but the generator doesn't place them: measured on belts, portals only made shortcuts and cracked tiles changed nothing.

Playing: ConveyorRules

The rules a game plays by. They are the same rules the solver counted with, so a game built on them always agrees with the par.

MethodWhat it does
ConveyorPlay NewGame(ConveyorData m)The starting position.
bool TryMove(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, int dir, List<int> path, out string reason) Moves the piece and updates the position in place: it rests where the ride ends, or Lost turns true when it ends in a hole, every gem it entered is taken, and resting on a switch turns the switched belts. path, when given, is cleared and gets every cell entered, in order, for a game to animate. On a refusal it returns false with a short reason, such as "an Obstacle stands on 23, right next to the piece". Overloads leave out the path, or the path and the reason.
ConveyorBlock Preview(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, int dir, out int end, out ConveyorEnd how, List<int> path = null) Where a move would end and how, without moving anything.
ConveyorBlock Blocked(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, int dir)Why a move can't start, without changing anything.
List<int> LegalMoves(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play)The directions a player can move now, moves into holes included. Empty when the puzzle is solved or the attempt is lost.
bool Hint(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, out int dir, int stateBudget = ConveyorSolver.DefaultBudget) The first move of a best way on from here. False when the puzzle is solved, the attempt is lost, or the goal can't be reached from here any more.
bool IsSolved(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play), bool IsLost(ConveyorPlay play) Won (resting on the goal with every gem taken), and lost (the piece entered a hole).
int BeltWay(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, int cell)Where the belt on a cell points now, with the switches as this position has them, or -1 when there is no belt there. Draw belts with this, not with the Arrow's stored Dir.
bool IsSwitch(ConveyorData m, int cell)Whether a cell is a switch.
bool IsTaken(m, play, cell), int PickupsLeft(m, play)How the gems stand in this position.
bool IsBroken(m, play, cell), int Twin(m, cell)For hand-made boards with cracked tiles or portals: whether a cracked tile is broken, and the other end of a portal (-1 when none).
bool Validate(ConveyorData m, out string error)Checks that the data makes sense: sizes, every listed cell on the board and in one list only, every belt pointing one of the four ways, the start and goal on floor and apart, switches on stop tiles and off the start and goal, switched belts on belts, and the limits on gems and switches. It doesn't say whether the puzzle can be solved (the solver does), nor whether a hand-made board keeps the belt rule.

ConveyorPlay is the position in play, and the only thing a game has to keep between moves:

MemberWhat it is
int CellWhere the piece is.
bool LostThe piece entered a hole. What happens next is your game's choice: the demo puts the position back to before that move.
int TakenThe gems taken, as a bit mask.
int SwitchThe switch state (0 at the start).
int BrokenCracked tiles broken, as a bit mask (hand-made boards only).
ConveyorPlay Clone()A copy, for undo.

Solving and rating

MemberWhat it does
ConveyorVerdict ConveyorSolver.Solve(ConveyorData m, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget) Solves a puzzle from its start.
ConveyorVerdict ConveyorSolver.SolveFrom(ConveyorData m, ConveyorPlay play, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget) Solves on from a position in play: its Par is the moves still needed, and a lost position has no way on.
ConveyorVerdict ConveyorSolver.Verify(ConveyorData m, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget) Solves and writes the verdict, the feel, the chest spots and the stars into the puzzle, with a note when the budget cut the search short. Use it after building or changing a puzzle by hand.
ConveyorSolver.DefaultBudget, ConveyorSolver.CountCap1,000,000 positions, and the 1,000,000,000 where the best-solution count stops.
ConveyorDifficulty.Score(m), Score(par, places, gems), Stars(score), Stars(m), Ceiling(stars), BandsThe stars: the score is the square root of par times resting places times (1 + gems / 4), read against the bands on the Generating page. Ceiling is the score a puzzle must stay under to have at most that many stars.

ConveyorVerdict holds Solvable, Par, Count, CountComplete, Unique, CanGetStuck, BudgetCut, Explored, Places, the dead-end, feel and chest-spot numbers that Verify copies into the puzzle, and one best solution as Moves (directions).

A plain belt board needs one position per cell. Each gem can double that, and switches multiply it by 2 or 4, so a board with 3 gems and Rotate switches can have 32 positions per cell. The generator only accepts puzzles whose search finished, and a solve you call yourself with too small a budget is marked as not proven, with a note.

A puzzle by hand

Fill in the board and the puzzle, check it with ConveyorRules.Validate, then let the solver write the verdict. On this 4 x 4 board, a move east from the start rides the belts along the bottom row, up the right side and back west onto the stop tile in the middle. A gem on the ice up the left side has to be fetched first. The comments show what it prints.

using System.Collections.Generic;
using System.Linq;
using SimpleConveyor;
using UnityEngine;

var m = new ConveyorData
{
    Width = 4, Height = 4,
    Start = 0,                                  // cell (0, 0), bottom left
    Goal = 15,                                  // cell (3, 3), top right
    Normal = new List<int> { 0, 10, 15 },       // stop tiles (the start and the goal are stop tiles too)
    Obstacles = new List<int> { 13 },           // a wall on (1, 3)
    Arrows = new List<ConveyorArrow>            // belts: a cell and the way it runs
    {
        new ConveyorArrow(1, Dirs.East), new ConveyorArrow(2, Dirs.East), new ConveyorArrow(3, Dirs.North),
        new ConveyorArrow(7, Dirs.North), new ConveyorArrow(11, Dirs.West),
    },
    Pickups = new List<int> { 8 },              // a gem on (0, 2); every cell in no list is ice
};

if (!ConveyorRules.Validate(m, out string error)) Debug.LogError(error);
ConveyorSolver.Verify(m);
Debug.Log(ConveyorAudit.Short(m));                                  // 4 x 4, par 5, 1 star
Debug.Log(string.Join(" ", m.Solution.Select(d => "NESW"[d])));     // N S E N E

var start = ConveyorRules.NewGame(m);
var path = new List<int>();
ConveyorRules.TryMove(m, start, Dirs.East, path, out _);
Debug.Log($"Rests on {start.Cell} after passing {string.Join(", ", path)}");   // Rests on 10 after passing 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 10

Without the gem the same board takes 3 moves (east, north, east), so the gem really is part of the puzzle: the piece slides north up the ice to fetch it and back down to the start before riding the belts home. The line has no "unique" because more than one route of 5 moves exists. Validate doesn't check the belt rule, so on a hand-made board it's up to you to keep belts from pointing into walls.

Presets, Auto and audit

MemberWhat it does
ConveyorPresets.AllThe 21 presets. Each ConveyorPreset has a Name ("Switches/Reverse"), a Description, and Make(), which returns fresh settings.
ConveyorSettings ConveyorPresets.Tune(ConveyorSettings s, List<string> changes) The Auto button: a copy with settings that fight each other fixed, and a line in changes for each fix.
bool ConveyorAudit.MeetsRequest(ConveyorData m)True when a puzzle got everything its settings asked for. A hand-made puzzle only needs to be proven and solvable.
List<string> ConveyorAudit.Missing(ConveyorData m)What it missed, in plain words, such as "1 of 2 gems needed by the best solution" or "4 stars, asked 3". Empty when it got everything.
bool ConveyorAudit.Verify(ConveyorData m, out string before, out string after)Solves again and restamps the verdict. Returns true when the verdict changed. Data that fails Validate is left alone, with the reason in after.
ConveyorData ConveyorAudit.Reroll(ConveyorData m)A fresh puzzle from the same settings and a new seed, or null for a hand-made one. An overload takes a cancel, progress and a time limit.
bool ConveyorAudit.Generated(ConveyorData m)True when the puzzle came from the generator (it has a seed and settings).
string ConveyorAudit.Short(ConveyorData m)One line, such as "8 x 8, par 17, 3 stars, unique".

Pictures

ConveyorExport draws a puzzle with no Unity code at all, so it also works in tools and on a server.

MemberWhat it does
byte[] Png(ConveyorData m, ExportOptions options = null)A .png file's bytes.
string Svg(ConveyorData m, ExportOptions options = null)An SVG document, which stays sharp at any size.
byte[] Pixels(ConveyorData m, ExportOptions options, out int width, out int height) Raw RGBA bytes, top row first. Another overload also gives a PictureFrame, whose CellAt(px, py) and CellCentre(cell, out px, out py) map a point on the picture to a cell and back.
int FitSize(ConveyorData m, ExportOptions options, int maxWidth, int maxHeight)The Size that fits a picture in a box.
ConveyorPlay PlayAt(ConveyorData m, int step)The position after the first step moves of the stored solution.
List<LegendIcon> Legend(ConveyorData m, int step, int iconPx, bool sideStops = false) The picture's legend as lines of text, each with its icon, for drawing beside the board in your own font.

ExportOptions has Style, Size (the board's longer side in pixels, 1600 by default), Step (-1 for the start, or a step of the solution, with the moves so far drawn as a line and the belts as the switches have turned them), Legend, Piece (false leaves the piece off, for a 3D view that puts a real one on top) and SideStops (marks the chest spots, off by default because on a printed puzzle they show where the route isn't).

TypeWhat it does
ConveyorSketchA puzzle as drawing marks in board units, which is what the pictures and the editor preview draw. Build(m, play, slides) gives one Mark per thing on the board (a Kind such as Arrow, Ground, Switch, Pickup or SideStop, its cell, numbers, and whether it is done or switched in that position) and the solution as Lines. Describe(m, cell, play) is a tooltip for one cell, and Frames(m) is the stored solution move by move, each with a caption.

ScriptableObjects

TypeWhat it holds
ConveyorPuzzleAssetOne puzzle: Puzzle and Label, plus shortcuts to its verdict (Width, Height, Solvable, IsUnique, OptimalCount, CanGetStuck, Par, Difficulty, Seed) and Set(data, label).
ConveyorPuzzlePackAn ordered list of puzzles: PackName, Puzzles, Count, Get(index) (null when out of range), Set, Add, Replace(index, data) and Clear().
PuzzleAssetsCreatePuzzle(data, label) and CreatePack(puzzles, packName) make new instances in memory, which you can save with AssetDatabase.CreateAsset in the editor or use at runtime.
IPuzzleProviderBoth asset types implement it: AppendTo(List<ConveyorData> levels) adds their puzzles in order, so one list in the inspector can take either.

Both are created by the Conveyor Generator's Save menu, not by an empty right-click Create.

Demo components

These are in SimpleConveyor.Demo. The Demos page explains how they fit together.

TypeWhat it does
ConveyorDemoThe one component the demo scene needs. It builds the board, game, HUD, hub, sounds and effects in code and runs the flow. Fields: Camera, UiThroughCamera, Pitch, HdrpLook and a level pack per mode (BeltsPack, GemsPack, SwitchesPack, IceAndHolesPack). Methods include Setup(), PlayEndless(mode, ice, stars), PlayLevel(mode, index), Generate(mode, settings), Begin(puzzle), NextGame() and BackToHub().
ConveyorGamePlays a puzzle on a ConveyorBoardView: moves against par, switches turning belts, falls, undo, restart, hints and solving by itself, with keyboard, swipe, tap and camera controls (KeyboardInput, PointerInput and CameraControls turn each off or on). Start a game with Begin(puzzle), drive it with Slide(dir), Push(screenDir), Tap(screenPoint), Undo(), Restart(), ShowHint() and StartAutoSolve(), read Moves, Falls, Solved and DeadEnd, and listen to Moved, Won, Stuck, Message, Cue and Moment. Why(m, play, dir, block) words a refusal for a player.
ConveyorBoardViewBuilds a puzzle in 3D from meshes made in code, with no prefabs: Build(puzzle), Show(play) (every gem taken or not, and every switched belt turned for that position), Idle(dt) (runs the belts), PlacePiece(local), FrameCamera(camera), CellWorld(cell) and LocalBounds. BeltsRun and BeltSpeed (0.6 cells a second) set how the belts run.
ConveyorHudThe in-game HUD and win panel, in uGUI with the built-in font. FreeAreas(camera) says where the board can go without the HUD covering it.
ConveyorHubThe front screen: the welcome panel, the mode cards, and each mode's Endless and Level Pack setup.
ConveyorDemoMode, ConveyorModes, ConveyorEndlessThe four demo modes (Belts, Gems, Switches, IceAndHoles), their names, colours and sample puzzles, and the Endless recipe for each mode, board and star count.
ConveyorPackProgressEach pack level's best moves, kept in PlayerPrefs: Best, Record, Clear and NameOf.
ConveyorCue, ConveyorMomentThe game's moments worth a sound (a move starting, a stop, a bump, a belt turning the piece (Arrow), a gem, a fall, a switch, a hint, a dead end, a rewind, the win, and Crack and Portal for hand-made boards), and the same with where it happened, its colour, its direction and, for the win, the board's extent.
ConveyorBeeperSounds made in code for every ConveyorCue, with no audio files.
ConveyorEffectsSmall effects (puffs, bursts, rings, chunks, swirls and confetti) from a pool of primitives, for every ConveyorMoment.
ConveyorMeshes, MeshBuilderThe meshes, made in code: the puck, the gem, chevrons and outlines in ConveyorMeshes, and MeshBuilder, which the board view uses for the tiles, walls, belts and switches.
DemoLook, DemoHdrpThe demo's colours, materials and the running belts' texture for the active render pipeline, and the HDRP volume that gives it the same look.

Building blocks

The belt builder itself is internal: every ConveyorGenerator method goes through it, so the belt rule and the checks always apply. Rng, the small deterministic random generator seeded from a string, is public for anyone who wants the same seeds in their own code.
