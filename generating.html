The Conveyor Generator window

Open Window > Living Failure > Simple Conveyor > Conveyor Generator. The settings are on the left in four sections (Board, Gimmicks, Chest spots and Quality), with Auto, Presets and Reset above them and the seed, batch and Generate controls below. The puzzles you've made are on the right, one at a time with the analysis pane, and the arrows page through them.

Every puzzle the window makes goes through verified generation: if a board misses what you asked for, the generator builds another from the next seed, and if none of its tries succeed it gives you the closest one with a note saying why. Generation runs on a background thread with a progress bar that says which board it is on and what it is doing, and a Cancel button that stops it within a fraction of a second, even in the middle of a board.

Each table below gives the label in the window, the matching field on ConveyorSettings for code, its range and its default. Values outside the range are clamped, so code can pass anything.

Gimmicks

Every gimmick is proven by the solver together with the rest of the board. Each switch and gem is needed by the best solution, and each hole is one that some ride of two or more cells ends in, so the player has to read the board to see it coming. Verified generation only accepts puzzles that have all of them.

SettingFieldRangeWhat it does
SwitchesSwitches0 to 2 (0)Stop tiles that change the switched belts each time the piece comes to rest on one. A second climb places them on the finished board and keeps only switches the best solution needs: made a plain stop tile, the goal can't be reached or the par gets longer. The belt rule holds in every switch state. Measured on 10 boards a row with 2 switches, they were needed on 59 of 60. Fewer fit on small boards.
Switch doesSwitchModeReverse, Rotate (Reverse)Reverse: the switched belts run the other way, and resting on a switch again turns them back (2 switch states). It adds about 1 move of par, but a player without a plan needs about 4 times the moves on 8 x 8. Rotate: they turn a quarter turn clockwise, and four rests bring them back (4 states). It adds 17 to 30% of par and about 24 times the moves for a player without a plan.
GemsPickups0 to 3 (0)Taken the moment a ride enters their cell, riding a belt across it or stopping on it, and the goal only counts once every one is taken. They go where they add the most moves, then the belts are climbed again around them, and only gems the best solution needs are kept (lifted, the par drops). Measured: 2 gems take 8 x 8 from a par of 17 to 36, and 3 take 6 x 6 from 10 to 25 and 10 x 10 from 26 to 69, so cap gem puzzles with Max par or a star target.
HolesHoles0 to 6 (0), needs IceCells that lose the attempt when a ride enters one (a game puts the piece back where it was). No belt ever points into a hole, so only an ice slide can carry the piece into one: without Ice none are placed, with a note, and Auto turns Ice on. Holes never change the par or the stars. Measured on 8 x 8 ice: about 4 holes and 5 rides into them, and a player without a plan needs about 3 times the moves.

Gems and switches cost search time. The solver tracks which gems are taken and which way the switches have turned the belts, so each gem can double the positions it searches, and switches multiply them by 2 (Reverse) or 4 (Rotate). Ice, walls and holes add none. Every preset still builds in under a second.

Chest spots

SettingFieldRangeWhat it does
Chest spotsSideStops0 to 40 (0)At least this many resting places off every best route: places a player can reach and still win from, but that no best solution passes. A game can put a chest, a key or anything a detour should pay for there. 0 means whatever the climb leaves. The climb meets this first, then makes the puzzle as hard as it can.

Saved puzzles list the spots in SideStops, and SideStopCosts gives the extra moves going by each one costs. The pane's Chest spots toggle marks them on the board and in exports. A belt-only board holds about as many resting places whatever you ask (about 21 on 8 x 8), so it has 3 to 7 chest spots anyway, and each one asked for past that costs about a move of par: 6 spots on 8 x 8 give a par near 14 instead of 17, and 10 give about 9. Ice boards have 16 (8 x 8) to 26 (10 x 10) anyway, and asking for 15 or 30 costs only about 4 moves of par (all measured).

How the stars work

Every proven puzzle gets a 1 to 5 star difficulty, or 0 when it isn't proven solvable. The score is the square root of the par times the number of resting places the piece can reach, where a place counts once in each switch state, and with gems the product is multiplied by 1 + gems / 4. The par is the effort, and the resting places are the room a player has to get lost in. The stars were fitted to 1,500 generated puzzles, each played by a simulated player who moves without a plan but prefers places it hasn't been yet, the same yardstick Simple Ice Slide's stars use, so a star means about the same in both.

The score tracks the simulated player with a rank correlation of 0.961, and the stars match the player's own stars on 73% of puzzles and are within one star on 96%.

Stars follow the board's size. Up to 6 x 6 gives 1 star (2 with ice at 6 x 6), 7 x 7 2, 8 x 8 3, 10 x 10 4, and 12 x 12 and up 5, and every size makes every level below its most. That is how Target stars aims, so for a target above your board's most, use a bigger board. Holes don't change the stars, and they are there to punish a careless ride.

Auto and presets

Auto fixes settings that fight each other and tells you what it changed. The window also shows what Auto would change, live, as a warning under the settings. In code it is ConveyorPresets.Tune. It:

  • turns Ice on when you ask for holes, because only an ice slide can carry the piece into one;
  • lowers Min par when it is at or over the most stop tiles the board may have, on boards without ice, switches or gems (there the piece only rests on stop tiles, each at most once on the best route);
  • lowers Chest spots on those same boards when the stop tiles left over by Min par can't hold that many;
  • lowers Min par when a 1 to 4 star target can never be met with it, on boards without gems (there the score is always more than the par);
  • raises Max par when it is too low for the stars you asked for on that size of board.

Presets replace every setting with a ready-made recipe. In code they are ConveyorPresets.All, and each one's Make() gives a fresh ConveyorSettings. Each was built 24 times for this page, and every one of the 504 puzzles got everything its recipe asked for. The par ranges are the widest seen over 48 builds of each.

Seeds, batches and saving

The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle. In the window, Random uses a fresh seed for every Generate and shows the one it used. Turn Random off to type a seed and keep it, and Copy copies it. Each puzzle in a batch gets its own seed, the batch's seed followed by a dash and the puzzle's number (-0, -1 and so on), and every puzzle stores its own seed, which the analysis pane can copy.

Verified generation tries the seed itself first, then the same seed with #1, #2 and so on added, until a puzzle meets the request. A puzzle's stored seed is the one that worked, such as level-7-5#1, so with the same settings it always rebuilds that exact puzzle.

Batch count makes 1 to 50 puzzles per click and adds them to the set. Clear on generate empties the set first, so each click replaces the batch. Time limit (0 to 600 seconds, 60 by default, 0 for none) is how long one puzzle may take: past it, generation keeps the closest board that finished, with a note, and if no board finished, that puzzle is listed as not made and the batch carries on. The limit is shared with the inspectors' Re-roll.

Only on target hides the fallbacks, and the cross removes a puzzle from the set. Save then writes Current as a ConveyorPuzzleAsset, or Pack (all) or Pack (on target only) as a ConveyorPuzzlePack, using the name in Save name as the suggestion.

Limits and notes

The generator never quietly gives you less than you asked for. Whenever something didn't go to plan, the puzzle's Notes say what happened, and the analysis pane shows every one. Each note starts with what it is about:

  • Target: no puzzle in the attempts had the stars you asked for. You got the closest, and when the target is outside what this board gave, the note says which way to change its size.
  • Verified: no puzzle in the attempts was proven solvable with no dead ends, inside your par window, with your chest spots, needed switches and gems, and live holes. It says what the closest one has and what to try: fewer of a gimmick, a bigger board, ice for more chest spots, a higher Max par, more climb steps or a bigger state budget.
  • Builder: you asked for holes without Ice, so none were placed, or the par ended under your Min par because this board size tops out lower.
  • Time limit: generation stopped at the time limit, and this is the closest of the boards that finished.
  • Solver: the state budget ran out before the search finished, or there are more than 1,000,000,000 best solutions and the count stops there.

Use ConveyorAudit.MeetsRequest(puzzle) to check whether a puzzle got everything its settings asked for, and ConveyorAudit.Missing(puzzle) to list what it missed. A pack's inspector can re-roll every puzzle in the pack that missed something (Re-roll > Fallbacks).

Generating from code

Everything the window does is available from code through ConveyorGenerator. Your settings object is copied and clamped first, so it is never changed.

using SimpleConveyor;

var s = new ConveyorSettings
{
    Width = 10, Height = 10,
    Switches = 1,                 // a switch the best solution needs
    Pickups = 1,                  // a gem to take before the goal counts
    TargetDifficulty = 4,
    Seed = "level-7",             // empty = a fresh random seed
};

ConveyorData one = ConveyorGenerator.Generate(s);            // one puzzle from this exact seed, solved and rated
ConveyorData good = ConveyorGenerator.GenerateVerified(s);   // tries seeds until the puzzle meets the request
List<ConveyorData> pack = ConveyorGenerator.GenerateBatch(s, 10);   // seeds level-7-0 to level-7-9, each verified

Generate, GenerateVerified and GenerateBatch each have an Async version that runs on a background thread and takes a CancellationToken. In Unity, await carries on on the main thread afterwards. GenerateBatchAsync also reports progress:

var progress = new Progress<int>(n => Debug.Log($"{n} of 10 done"));
List<ConveyorData> puzzles = await ConveyorGenerator.GenerateBatchAsync(s, 10, progress, cancel.Token);

For a time limit and a running report of what a board is doing, use GenerateVerified(settings, cancel, progress, timeLimitSeconds), which the window uses. See the API Reference.

How long it takes (measured on a desktop, 24 builds of each preset; a phone is slower): most presets take 5 to 300 milliseconds a puzzle. The slowest builds were 0.5 to 0.9 seconds, with Rotate switches, holes on a 10 x 10 ice board, or a star target that took several tries. In a game, generate with the Async versions (the demo does) or ahead of time in the editor as a pack.
