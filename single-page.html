Simple Conveyor

Conveyor belt puzzles for Unity that are generated, solved exactly and rated.

Simple Conveyor builds a conveyor belt puzzle from your settings, then proves it can be solved and works out its par, the fewest moves it takes. The player pushes the piece off a stop tile, and the belts carry it their way until it comes to rest on another one, so the puzzle is reading where each ride ends. It can add ice, switches that turn belts, gems to collect and holes, and it can keep resting places off the best route for chests or keys. Every puzzle is rated 1 to 5 stars and can be saved as a ScriptableObject, exported as a PNG or SVG picture, or played in the included 3D demo.

Version 1.0, by Living Failure

What it is

Simple Conveyor is a conveyor belt puzzle generator with a solver built in. A piece rests on a stop tile, and the player sends it north, east, south or west. It moves onto the next cell, and from there every belt it enters carries it on the way that belt points, turning it where the belts turn, until it reaches a stop tile and rests. You choose the board and what goes on it, and it builds a puzzle for you. Then it searches every position the puzzle can reach, so you know for certain that it can be solved and how many moves the best solution takes.

Every generated board keeps one hard rule: no belt ever points into a wall, a hole or off the board, in any switch state. A belt always carries the piece onto another belt, ice or a stop tile, so the player never watches a ride end against a wall for no reason, and no ride goes round for ever.

Everything is deterministic. The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle, and every puzzle keeps the settings and seed it was made from. When the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it says so in the puzzle's notes instead of quietly handing you something else. In the editor pictures and in the demo every kind of tile has its own shape as well as its own colour, so colour-blind players can tell them apart.

The rules

Belts carry the piece

A belt carries the piece one cell its way, onto the next belt or tile, so a ride follows the belts until it ends. A move into a wall or the board's edge right next to the piece doesn't happen.

Stop tiles stop it

The piece rests on the first stop tile a ride reaches. Stop tiles are never side by side, so every move rides at least one belt (or ice). The start and the goal are stop tiles too.

Ice keeps it going

With Ice on, ice keeps the piece going the way it was moving until a stop tile stops it, a belt turns it, or a wall or the edge leaves it resting on the ice in front.

Reach the goal

Rest on the goal to win. With gems on the board, the goal only counts once the piece has taken every one, by riding across it or stopping on it.

Switches turn belts

Each time the piece comes to rest on a switch, every switched belt turns: Reverse makes them run the other way, Rotate turns them a quarter turn clockwise.

Avoid the holes

On ice boards, a ride that ends in a hole loses the attempt. What that means is up to your game: the demo plays the fall and puts the piece back where it was.

A move the rules don't allow simply doesn't happen, and the rules say why. The solver and your game use the same rules, so they always agree.

Features

Proven par

The solver searches every position the puzzle can reach, so the par is the true minimum and not an estimate. It also counts how many best solutions there are.

Difficulty in stars

A 1 to 5 star rating from the par and the number of places the piece can come to rest, which is the room a player has to get lost in. Ask for a star count and the generator keeps trying until it gets one.

Board size

Boards from 4 to 32 cells each way, with stop tiles on up to 15 to 50% of the board, walls on up to 15%, and ice patches if you want them.

The belt rule

No belt points into a wall, a hole or off the board, in any switch state, and no ride goes round for ever. Nothing the piece can reach is a dead end.

Gimmicks

Up to 2 switches that reverse or rotate belts, 3 gems to collect, and 6 holes on ice boards.

Every gimmick matters

Each switch and gem is needed by the best solution. Make a switch a plain stop tile and the puzzle gets longer or can't be solved, and lift a gem and it gets shorter. Each hole is one that some ride of two or more cells really ends in.

Chest spots

Ask for up to 40 resting places off every best route, where a game can put a chest, a key or anything a detour should pay for. Each one comes with the extra moves going by it costs.

Printable export

Save any puzzle as a PNG or SVG: the colour view, a black on white printable sheet that draws every tile by its shape alone, or an answer key with the best solution drawn on.

ScriptableObjects

Save single puzzles or ordered packs, with inspectors that preview, re-check and regenerate them.

Play rules included

ConveyorRules tells your game what a move does and every cell it passes through, using the same rules the solver counted with, so the game and the par always agree. It also gives hints from any position.

Editor tooling

One Conveyor Generator window with 21 presets, an Auto button that fixes settings that fight each other, and generation on a background thread with a progress bar, Cancel and a time limit. Step through the best solution move by move on the board.

A 3D demo

Four game modes with endless puzzles and 42 ready-made levels, running belts, sounds and effects, all built in code. It works in URP, HDRP and the Built-in pipeline.

How it works

1

Configure

Pick the board, the gimmicks, the chest spots and the difficulty, in the Conveyor Generator window or with a ConveyorSettings object in code.

2

Lay the belts

Stop tiles go down first, never side by side. Every other cell becomes a belt, grown outward from a stop tile, so following the belts always ends on a stop tile.

3

Pick the start and goal

They go on the pair of stop tiles furthest apart in moves, where nothing the piece can reach is a dead end.

4

Climb

One change at a time: a belt points another way, a belt becomes a stop tile or back, a wall comes or goes, and (with Ice) a belt becomes ice or back. The change is kept when the puzzle doesn't get worse. A change that would break the belt rule or leave a dead end is never kept. With a star target, the climb stops short of the next star level.

5

Add the gimmicks

Switches, holes and gems each get a second climb on the finished board. Switches and gems go where the best solution needs them, and holes where a ride of two or more cells ends in them.

6

Rate and check

The finished puzzle is solved again from its start. It records the par, one best solution, how many best solutions exist, the chest spots, and the stars. A puzzle that misses what you asked for is built again from the next seed.

7

Use it

Save it as an asset, export a picture of it, play it in the demo, or read the ConveyorData in your own scripts.

Why "verified"

Belt puzzles are easy to get wrong by hand. A belt can point into a wall, two belts can pass the piece back and forth for ever, a shortcut can skip the whole route you designed, and one move can strand the piece for good. Simple Conveyor proves the things you design around:

  • The par is real. It is the shortest solution that exists, so a 20-move puzzle really takes 20 moves.
  • Every position is searched. A puzzle is only accepted when the search covered every position it can reach, in every switch state and with every set of gems taken.
  • No dead ends. Nothing the piece can reach is a place it can never win from.
  • The belt rule holds. No belt points into a wall, a hole or off the board, whichever way the switches have turned it.
  • Gimmicks earn their place. A switch or gem the best solution could do without isn't left on the board, and every hole is one that a ride of two or more cells really ends in.
  • Misses are reported. When a star count, a par window or a gimmick that doesn't fit can't be met, the puzzle's notes say what happened and what you got instead.

Two honest limits: stars follow the board's size (up to 6 x 6 gives 1 star, 7 x 7 2, 8 x 8 3, 10 x 10 4, and 12 x 12 and up 5), so a target far from the size comes back as the closest with a note. And gems lengthen puzzles fast: 3 gems take a 10 x 10 board from a par of about 26 to about 69, so cap gem puzzles with a maximum par or a star target.

Quick Start

From import to a puzzle you can play, in about five minutes.

1. Install

Import the package. Everything lives under Assets/SimpleConveyor/, and nothing is added to your scenes until you ask for it.

The core (the generator, the solver, the play rules and the puzzle data) has no package dependencies. The demo uses the Input System and Unity UI packages. It reads the mouse, touch and keyboard through the Input System, so set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both before you play it.

Render pipelines: URP is recommended, HDRP is supported and the Built-in pipeline still works. The demo builds its materials from the active pipeline's default shader and keeps that shader in your builds, and on HDRP it adds a small volume so it looks the same as on the others (turn off HdrpLook on the ConveyorDemo component to use your own lighting instead). Simple Conveyor was built and tested with Unity 6000.6.

2. Generate a puzzle

Open Window > Living Failure > Simple Conveyor > Conveyor Generator. You can also get there from Assets > Create > Simple Conveyor > Conveyor Generator.

1

Take a preset

Press Presets and pick Belts/First puzzle: a 5 x 5 board of belts and stop tiles with a par of 6 or 7. A preset replaces every setting with a ready-made recipe.

2

Generate

Press Generate. Puzzles are built and solved on a background thread, so the window stays responsive, and a long batch shows a progress bar with a Cancel button. Raise Batch count to make up to 50 at a time.

3

Try a few more

Switches/Reverse adds 2 switches that make the switched belts run the other way. Gems/Small adds a gem to take before the goal counts. Holes/Medium lays ice with 3 holes an ice slide can carry the piece into, and Chest spots/Belts keeps at least 6 resting places off the best route for chests or keys.

If you change settings by hand and they fight each other (holes without ice, a par window the stop tiles can never give, more chest spots than the board can hold, or a par window the target stars can never meet), the window warns you, and Auto fixes them and tells you what it changed.

3. Read the analysis

The pane on the right shows the selected puzzle. From the top:

  • The verdict: the par in moves (and "one best solution" when there is just one) and the difficulty out of 5, with a line that sums up the board and its seed. A warning shows when the piece can get stuck.
  • Board: a picture of the puzzle with a Move slider above it that steps through the best solution, and Play to watch it move by move. The piece rides, gems go, switched belts turn and the moves so far are drawn as a line. Hover a cell to read it, and zoom in on a big board. Chest spots marks every resting place off the best route with the extra moves going by it costs.
  • On this board: beside the picture, a plain-words list of what the board holds.
  • Export: save the puzzle as a PNG or SVG, as the colour view, a printable sheet or an answer key.
  • Best solution: every move written out, with a Copy solution button.
  • Facts and Generation report: the numbers (resting places, choices, decoys, wrong turns, chest spots and more) and every note the generator left.

Pressing Play is the quickest way to judge a puzzle. You can see where each ride ends and how the belts carry the piece round the board to reach the goal.

4. Save it

Press Save and choose:

  • Current saves the selected puzzle as a ConveyorPuzzleAsset.
  • Pack (all) saves every puzzle in the set as one ordered ConveyorPuzzlePack.
  • Pack (on target only) leaves out the fallbacks, the puzzles that missed something their settings asked for.

Both asset types have inspectors with the same analysis pane, plus Verify, which solves the puzzle again and refreshes its stored verdict, and Re-roll, which builds a new puzzle from the same settings with a fresh seed. A pack can also re-roll just its fallbacks, and Export all writes every puzzle and its answer key into a folder you pick.

5. Play the demo

Open Assets/SimpleConveyor/Scenes/SimpleConveyorDemo.unity and press Play. The demo opens on a welcome screen. Press Let's play, pick a mode, then choose Endless for a new puzzle to your order or Level Pack for the ready-made levels.

The controls are also listed at the bottom of the screen while you play:

  • Arrow keys or WASD move the puck, and so does a swipe. A tap beside the puck moves it toward the tap. Each key moves the way that looks closest to it on screen, so the controls stay right however the camera is turned.
  • U (or Backspace, or Ctrl+Z) undoes a move, R restarts and H shows a hint.
  • F lets the solver play the rest of the puzzle from where you are, and F or R stops it.
  • [ and ] turn the camera, Home and End tilt it, the mouse wheel or + and - zoom, and right-drag turns and tilts. V resets the view.
  • M mutes the sound and Esc goes back to the menu.

The same actions are on the buttons at the bottom: Undo, Restart, Hint, Solve and Menu. A ride into a hole plays out, the puck drops in, and then it goes back where it was. A fall isn't counted as a move.

6. Use it in your game

Make a puzzle in code, check what the solver found, and read the rest straight off the ConveyorData:

using SimpleConveyor;

var settings = new ConveyorSettings
{
    Width = 8, Height = 8,
    Pickups = 1,                 // a gem to take before the goal counts
    TargetDifficulty = 3,        // keep trying seeds until it rates 3 stars
};
ConveyorData puzzle = ConveyorGenerator.GenerateVerified(settings);
Debug.Log($"Par {puzzle.Par} moves, {puzzle.Difficulty} stars, solvable: {puzzle.Solvable}");

To run your own game on it, keep a ConveyorPlay and ask ConveyorRules what each move does. These are the same rules the solver used, so your game always agrees with the par:

ConveyorPlay play = ConveyorRules.NewGame(puzzle);
if (ConveyorRules.TryMove(puzzle, play, 1, out string reason))   // 0 north, 1 east, 2 south, 3 west
{
    if (play.Lost) Debug.Log("Into a hole!");
    else if (ConveyorRules.IsSolved(puzzle, play)) Debug.Log("Solved!");
}
else Debug.Log(reason);                                           // why not: a wall or the edge right next to the piece

if (ConveyorRules.Hint(puzzle, play, out int dir))
    Debug.Log($"A best move from here: {dir}. {ConveyorRules.PickupsLeft(puzzle, play)} gems left.");

The core numbers cells from the bottom left, row by row (y * Width + x), and names them by what they do (see Names in the data), so a refused move's reason reads like "an Obstacle stands on 23, right next to the piece". Another overload of TryMove also lists every cell the ride passes through, for a game to animate. The demo turns all of this into words and motion for players.

Or let the demo's 3D view draw and play it for you. ConveyorGame adds a ConveyorBoardView to the same object and builds the whole board from code:

using SimpleConveyor.Demo;

var game = new GameObject("Conveyor").AddComponent<ConveyorGame>();
game.Begin(puzzle);
game.View.FrameCamera(Camera.main);

A saved puzzle works the same way: give your script a ConveyorPuzzleAsset field and use its Puzzle, or a ConveyorPuzzlePack and its Get(index). See the API Reference for the rest.

The Conveyor Generator window

How the stars work

Every proven puzzle gets a 1 to 5 star difficulty, or 0 when it isn't proven solvable. The score is the square root of the par times the number of resting places the piece can reach, where a place counts once in each switch state, and with gems the product is multiplied by 1 + gems / 4. The par is the effort, and the resting places are the room a player has to get lost in. The stars were fitted to 1,500 generated puzzles, each played by a simulated player who moves without a plan but prefers places it hasn't been yet, the same yardstick Simple Ice Slide's stars use, so a star means about the same in both.

Stars follow the board's size. Up to 6 x 6 gives 1 star (2 with ice at 6 x 6), 7 x 7 2, 8 x 8 3, 10 x 10 4, and 12 x 12 and up 5, and every size makes every level below its most. That is how Target stars aims, so for a target above your board's most, use a bigger board. Holes don't change the stars, and they are there to punish a careless ride.

Auto and presets

Auto fixes settings that fight each other and tells you what it changed. The window also shows what Auto would change, live, as a warning under the settings. In code it is ConveyorPresets.Tune. It:

  • turns Ice on when you ask for holes, because only an ice slide can carry the piece into one;
  • lowers Min par when it is at or over the most stop tiles the board may have, on boards without ice, switches or gems (there the piece only rests on stop tiles, each at most once on the best route);
  • lowers Chest spots on those same boards when the stop tiles left over by Min par can't hold that many;
  • lowers Min par when a 1 to 4 star target can never be met with it, on boards without gems (there the score is always more than the par);
  • raises Max par when it is too low for the stars you asked for on that size of board.

Presets replace every setting with a ready-made recipe. In code they are ConveyorPresets.All, and each one's Make() gives a fresh ConveyorSettings. Seeds, batches and saving

The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle. In the window, Random uses a fresh seed for every Generate and shows the one it used. Turn Random off to type a seed and keep it, and Copy copies it. Each puzzle in a batch gets its own seed, the batch's seed followed by a dash and the puzzle's number (-0, -1 and so on), and every puzzle stores its own seed, which the analysis pane can copy.

Verified generation tries the seed itself first, then the same seed with #1, #2 and so on added, until a puzzle meets the request. A puzzle's stored seed is the one that worked, such as level-7-5#1, so with the same settings it always rebuilds that exact puzzle.

Batch count makes 1 to 50 puzzles per click and adds them to the set. Clear on generate empties the set first, so each click replaces the batch. Time limit (0 to 600 seconds, 60 by default, 0 for none) is how long one puzzle may take: past it, generation keeps the closest board that finished, with a note, and if no board finished, that puzzle is listed as not made and the batch carries on. The limit is shared with the inspectors' Re-roll.

Only on target hides the fallbacks, and the cross removes a puzzle from the set. Save then writes Current as a ConveyorPuzzleAsset, or Pack (all) or Pack (on target only) as a ConveyorPuzzlePack, using the name in Save name as the suggestion.

Limits and notes

The generator never quietly gives you less than you asked for. Whenever something didn't go to plan, the puzzle's Notes say what happened, and the analysis pane shows every one. Each note starts with what it is about:

  • Target: no puzzle in the attempts had the stars you asked for. You got the closest, and when the target is outside what this board gave, the note says which way to change its size.
  • Verified: no puzzle in the attempts was proven solvable with no dead ends, inside your par window, with your chest spots, needed switches and gems, and live holes. It says what the closest one has and what to try: fewer of a gimmick, a bigger board, ice for more chest spots, a higher Max par, more climb steps or a bigger state budget.
  • Builder: you asked for holes without Ice, so none were placed, or the par ended under your Min par because this board size tops out lower.
  • Time limit: generation stopped at the time limit, and this is the closest of the boards that finished.
  • Solver: the state budget ran out before the search finished, or there are more than 1,000,000,000 best solutions and the count stops there.

Use ConveyorAudit.MeetsRequest(puzzle) to check whether a puzzle got everything its settings asked for, and ConveyorAudit.Missing(puzzle) to list what it missed. A pack's inspector can re-roll every puzzle in the pack that missed something (Re-roll > Fallbacks).

Generating from code

Everything the window does is available from code through ConveyorGenerator. Your settings object is copied and clamped first, so it is never changed.

using SimpleConveyor;

var s = new ConveyorSettings
{
    Width = 10, Height = 10,
    Switches = 1,                 // a switch the best solution needs
    Pickups = 1,                  // a gem to take before the goal counts
    TargetDifficulty = 4,
    Seed = "level-7",             // empty = a fresh random seed
};

ConveyorData one = ConveyorGenerator.Generate(s);            // one puzzle from this exact seed, solved and rated
ConveyorData good = ConveyorGenerator.GenerateVerified(s);   // tries seeds until the puzzle meets the request
List<ConveyorData> pack = ConveyorGenerator.GenerateBatch(s, 10);   // seeds level-7-0 to level-7-9, each verified

Generate, GenerateVerified and GenerateBatch each have an Async version that runs on a background thread and takes a CancellationToken. In Unity, await carries on on the main thread afterwards. GenerateBatchAsync also reports progress:

var progress = new Progress<int>(n => Debug.Log($"{n} of 10 done"));
List<ConveyorData> puzzles = await ConveyorGenerator.GenerateBatchAsync(s, 10, progress, cancel.Token);

For a time limit and a running report of what a board is doing, use GenerateVerified(settings, cancel, progress, timeLimitSeconds), which the window uses. See the API Reference.

How long it takes (measured on a desktop, 24 builds of each preset; a phone is slower): most presets take 5 to 300 milliseconds a puzzle. The slowest builds were 0.5 to 0.9 seconds, with Rotate switches, holes on a 10 x 10 ice board, or a star target that took several tries. In a game, generate with the Async versions (the demo does) or ahead of time in the editor as a pack.

  • Directions are 0 North (y + 1), 1 East (x + 1), 2 South and 3 West. Dirs has them as North, East, South, West and Count, with Dx(dir), Dy(dir), Opposite(dir) and Name(dir).

A puzzle by hand

Fill in the board and the puzzle, check it with ConveyorRules.Validate, then let the solver write the verdict. On this 4 x 4 board, a move east from the start rides the belts along the bottom row, up the right side and back west onto the stop tile in the middle. A gem on the ice up the left side has to be fetched first. The comments show what it prints.

using System.Collections.Generic;
using System.Linq;
using SimpleConveyor;
using UnityEngine;

var m = new ConveyorData
{
    Width = 4, Height = 4,
    Start = 0,                                  // cell (0, 0), bottom left
    Goal = 15,                                  // cell (3, 3), top right
    Normal = new List<int> { 0, 10, 15 },       // stop tiles (the start and the goal are stop tiles too)
    Obstacles = new List<int> { 13 },           // a wall on (1, 3)
    Arrows = new List<ConveyorArrow>            // belts: a cell and the way it runs
    {
        new ConveyorArrow(1, Dirs.East), new ConveyorArrow(2, Dirs.East), new ConveyorArrow(3, Dirs.North),
        new ConveyorArrow(7, Dirs.North), new ConveyorArrow(11, Dirs.West),
    },
    Pickups = new List<int> { 8 },              // a gem on (0, 2); every cell in no list is ice
};

if (!ConveyorRules.Validate(m, out string error)) Debug.LogError(error);
ConveyorSolver.Verify(m);
Debug.Log(ConveyorAudit.Short(m));                                  // 4 x 4, par 5, 1 star
Debug.Log(string.Join(" ", m.Solution.Select(d => "NESW"[d])));     // N S E N E

var start = ConveyorRules.NewGame(m);
var path = new List<int>();
ConveyorRules.TryMove(m, start, Dirs.East, path, out _);
Debug.Log($"Rests on {start.Cell} after passing {string.Join(", ", path)}");   // Rests on 10 after passing 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 10

Without the gem the same board takes 3 moves (east, north, east), so the gem really is part of the puzzle: the piece slides north up the ice to fetch it and back down to the start before riding the belts home. The line has no "unique" because more than one route of 5 moves exists. Validate doesn't check the belt rule, so on a hand-made board it's up to you to keep belts from pointing into walls.

What the 3D demo does, how it is put together, and how to use its pieces in your own game.

The demo scene

Your own packs: make puzzles in the Conveyor Generator, choose Save > Pack (all), and drag the pack into a slot on the ConveyorDemo component.

Progress: each level's best number of moves is saved in PlayerPrefs under SimpleConveyor.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>, so it stays on that device. The win panel says "Level complete!", "New best" or "Your best is" accordingly, and a level Solve helped with isn't recorded ("Solve helped, so this one isn't saved").

Playing

The controls are listed at the bottom of the screen while you play, and in the Quick Start. A few details:

  • Moving: the arrow keys, WASD and swipes move the puck the way that looks closest on screen, so the controls stay right however the camera is turned. A tap beside the puck moves it toward the tap. A key pressed mid-ride waits for the ride to end. The puck glides at about 0.07 seconds a cell, turning wherever a belt turns it.
  • The board reacts: the belts run all the time, their chevrons moving the way each belt carries the puck. When the puck comes to rest on a switch, every belt with yellow corners turns round. A gem spins up and away as the puck rides over it, leaving its outline behind.
  • A fall: a ride that ends in a hole plays out, the puck drops in, and then it goes back to where it was before that move. A fall isn't counted as a move, and the card counts the falls.
  • A refused move nudges the puck toward that way, and the card says why in plain words: "That's the edge of the board." or "A wall is in the way." Reaching the goal with gems left says how many are left.
  • Undo puts the board back as it was before the last move (a gem back, the switched belts turned back) and moves the puck back the way it came. Restart pops the puck back in on its start.
  • Hint floats a purple arrow over the puck the way a best solution moves next. Solve (or F) plays the rest by itself, one best move at a time. The button says Stop meanwhile, and F or R stops it.
  • The view: the camera frames the board in the space the HUD leaves free, in landscape or portrait, and again whenever the window changes shape. Turn, tilt and zoom it with the keys or the mouse, and when zoomed in it follows the puck.
  • The rating on the win panel is 3 pips on par, 2 a little over (up to 2 moves, or a quarter of the par on longer puzzles) and 1 beyond that.

Sounds and effects

ConveyorBeeper makes every sound in code when the demo starts: a swish as a move starts and a soft thud as the puck stops (a few versions of each, so a run of moves doesn't sound robotic), a dull bump for a refused move, a soft low clack when a belt turns the puck (belts turn it often, so it stays quiet), a clunk and a rising whirr when a switch turns the belts, a sparkle for a gem, a falling plop for a fall, a ping for a hint, a rewind for undo and restart, a win arpeggio and a button blip. M mutes it. There is no music.

ConveyorEffects adds small effects made from a pool of tiny cubes: a puff behind the puck as it sets off and ahead of it as it stops or bumps, a small puff where a belt turns it, a magenta ring and burst round a switch, an amber and white burst from a gem, a ring round a hole the puck falls into, and confetti over the whole board for the win. Nothing is created or destroyed while you play, and because the effects use the demo's ordinary materials, they look the same in every render pipeline.

Render pipelines

The demo works in URP (recommended), HDRP and the Built-in pipeline without any changes. Its materials use the active pipeline's default lit shader, including the belts' running texture, and a build step keeps that shader in your builds, so the demo doesn't draw pink on a phone.

HDRP lights in physical units, sets the exposure on its own and takes its ambient light from a sky, so the demo would come out dark and tinted. With HdrpLook on, DemoHdrp adds a global volume with a fixed exposure, no tone mapping or bloom, and a gradient sky in the demo's colours, clears the camera to the demo's background colour, and sets the sun in lux. That code only compiles when the HDRP package is installed.

The shipped scene was checked in all three pipelines: every material on the pipeline's lit shader, nothing pink, the belts running, every level played to par, and the pictures within a couple of shades of each other.

Using the pieces yourself

The demo components work on their own too. The smallest setup is one ConveyorGame, which adds its ConveyorBoardView automatically:

using SimpleConveyor;
using SimpleConveyor.Demo;

var game = new GameObject("Conveyor").AddComponent<ConveyorGame>();
game.Begin(puzzle);                                    // build the board and start a game
game.View.FrameCamera(Camera.main, pitch: 50f);        // the camera is yours: frame the board once
game.Message += text => Debug.Log(text);               // a refused move or a fall, in the player's words
game.Won += g => Debug.Log($"Solved in {g.Moves} moves, par {g.Par}");
game.Slide(Dirs.East);                                 // move from your own code (keys, swipes and taps work too)
  • Input: ConveyorGame reads the keys, swipes and taps itself while it is enabled. To drive it yourself, turn off KeyboardInput, PointerInput or both. Your code can move either way, with Slide(dir), Push(screenDirection) and Tap(screenPoint), and call Undo(), Restart(), ShowHint() and StartAutoSolve(). Set PointerBlocked so a press over your own UI doesn't move the puck.
  • The camera is yours unless you turn on CameraControls, which lets the game frame the board and gives players the turn, tilt and zoom controls. FrameAreas keeps the board in the parts of the screen your UI leaves free.
  • Events: Moved after every change of position, Won once when it is solved, Stuck when a move leaves no way to the goal (a generated puzzle never does that), Message for things a player should read, Cue for every moment worth a sound, and Moment for the same moments with a position and colour for effects.
  • Falls: the game puts the position back after a fall. If your game wants a fall to cost more, listen for ConveyorCue.Fall on Cue and do what you like, such as calling Restart().
  • A HUD: add a ConveyorHud and set its Game, or read Moves, Par, Falls, Solved, HintsUsed and CanUndo into your own UI.
  • The look: the colours, materials and the belt texture are in DemoLook.cs, the meshes in ConveyorMeshes.cs and ConveyorBoardView.cs. CellTime sets how long the puck takes to cross a cell (0.07 seconds by default), SwitchTurnTime how long switched belts take to turn (0.35), and the board view's BeltSpeed and BeltsRun how the belts run.
  • Without the 3D view: keep a ConveyorPlay and use ConveyorRules directly, as in the Quick Start.

Troubleshooting

What the notes mean, why a request can miss, and how long things take.

Reading the notes

Whenever the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it writes a note on the puzzle instead of quietly giving you less. The analysis pane shows every note, and so does ConveyorData.Notes from code. Each one starts with what it is about, and the Generating page lists them all. ConveyorAudit.MeetsRequest(puzzle) tells you whether a puzzle got everything its settings asked for, ConveyorAudit.Missing(puzzle) lists what it missed, and a pack's Re-roll > Fallbacks replaces the ones that missed.

"Target: 5 stars not found in 40 tries; the closest has 4."

The stars follow the board's size, because a bigger board holds a longer par and more places to get lost. When your target is outside what the board gave, the note says so and which way to change its size. Up to 6 x 6 gives 1 star (2 with ice at 6 x 6), 7 x 7 2, 8 x 8 3, 10 x 10 4, and 12 x 12 and up 5, and every size makes every level below its most. Also:

  • raise Max attempts under Advanced, so more seeds are tried;
  • remember that gems raise the score: it is multiplied by 1 + gems / 4, and gems lengthen the par a lot, so a gem puzzle rates higher than a plain one on the same board. Use a smaller board for a low target with gems;
  • a tight Max par can keep a puzzle below the stars you asked for. Auto raises it when it can never reach them.

"Verified: no puzzle in 40 tries was proven solvable with no dead ends and a par of ..."

No board met the hard requirements: proven solvable with no dead ends, inside your par window, with your chest spots, every switch and gem needed, and the live holes asked for. The note lists what it was looking for, what the closest board has, and what to try: fewer of a gimmick, a bigger board, ice for more chest spots, a higher Max par, more climb steps or a bigger state budget.

Auto fixes the settings known to fight each other, and the window shows what it would change before you press it.

Fewer gimmicks than asked

Every switch and gem has to be needed by the best solution, and every hole has to end some ride of two or more cells, so one that would change nothing isn't kept. Verified generation tries more seeds until a puzzle has them all, and the window's Only on target keeps just the puzzles that got everything. When none of the tries does, the "Verified:" note says how many the closest one has.

  • Switches: two needed switches need room, so fewer fit on small boards, and a low star target can leave no room for a second. Use a bigger board or one switch.
  • Gems: every gem adds moves, so a tight Max par or a low star target can leave room for fewer. Raise Max par, raise the target or use a bigger board.
  • Holes: only an ice slide can carry the piece into a hole, so a board with little ice has room for few. Use a bigger board or fewer holes.

No holes on the board

Holes need Ice. No belt ever points into a hole, so without ice the only way in would be pushing straight into one from next to it, which isn't a puzzle. A board asked for holes without ice gets none, with the note "Builder: no holes: holes need Ice", and Auto turns Ice on.

Few chest spots

On a belts-only board the piece only rests on stop tiles, and most of them are on the best route, so an 8 x 8 board has only about 3 to 7 chest spots of its own. Asking for more works, but each one past that costs about a move of par, and past the stop tiles the board can have, it can't be met (Auto lowers it). Turn on Ice for many more: ice boards have about 16 (8 x 8) to 26 (10 x 10) anyway.

A hand-made board misbehaves

The generator keeps its rules on every board it makes, but a board you build by hand only has to pass ConveyorRules.Validate, which checks the data, not the design:

  • A belt pointing into a wall or off the board stops the piece on that belt. The rules play it correctly, but it looks like a broken belt, which is why the generator never makes one.
  • Belts that pass the piece round in a circle make that move refused ("That move would never end"), and nothing moves.
  • "Can get stuck": a hand-made board can have dead ends. Check CanGetStuck after ConveyorSolver.Verify, and give players Undo and Restart, as the demo does. Its card also says when there is no way to finish from where the player is.

Generation feels slow

Measured on a desktop PC outside Unity, 24 builds of each preset (your machine and Unity's runtime will differ, and a phone is slower):

PuzzleTime for one verified puzzle
Belts presets, 5 x 5 to 14 x 145 to 125 ms
Difficulty presets (a star target)22 to 680 ms
Ice and chest spots10 to 121 ms
Gems12 to 152 ms
Switches26 to 477 ms
Holes on ice27 to 854 ms

A star target that takes several tries is the usual slow case. The demo's Endless recipes, measured the same way, took up to about 2.3 seconds.

  • In the window, generation runs on a background thread with a progress bar that says what the board is doing, and Cancel stops it within a fraction of a second. The Time limit (60 seconds by default) keeps the closest board that finished, with a "Time limit:" note.
  • In a game, use the Async methods, as the demo does for Endless, or make levels ahead of time in the editor and ship them as a pack.
  • On WebGL there are no threads, so the demo makes Endless puzzles on the main thread there, and the game pauses while it does.

"Solver: ..."

The solver searches up to State budget positions (1,000,000 by default). A plain belt board needs one per cell, each gem can double that and switches multiply it by 2 or 4, so even a 32 x 32 board with 3 gems and Rotate switches stays far below the budget. You would only see this note on a hand-made board with many gems or cracked tiles, or with a very small budget. Raise the State budget under Advanced (up to 5,000,000), or pass a bigger budget to ConveyorSolver.Verify and ConveyorRules.Hint.

"Solver: more than 1,000,000,000 best solutions; the count stops there." only means the count is capped.

The demo ignores taps or keys

The demo uses the Input System package. Set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both. The hub makes an EventSystem with the Input System's UI module when the scene has none. If your scene already has an EventSystem, its input module has to match your input handling. Presses on the HUD's card and buttons never move the puck.

Pink or dark in a pipeline

  • Pink in a build: the demo makes its materials in code, and a build strips shaders that only code uses. Before every build, ConveyorLookKeeper writes Runtime/Demo/Resources/SimpleConveyorLook.mat with the active pipeline's lit shader, so the build keeps it. If you see pink, check that file exists after a build (deleting it is fine: the next build writes it again).
  • Dark or tinted in HDRP: out of the box HDRP uses physical light units, automatic exposure and sky lighting. ConveyorDemo.HdrpLook (on by default) adds a global volume at priority 10 with a fixed exposure and a gradient sky, and sets the sun in lux, so the demo looks the same as on the other pipelines. Turn it off to let your own volumes and lights take over.

Resetting the demo's progress

The demo saves each pack level's best moves in PlayerPrefs, under SimpleConveyor.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>. Call ConveyorPackProgress.Clear(packName, count) to forget a pack, or delete those keys. A pack's progress is filed under its pack name, so renaming a pack starts it fresh.

Seeds and versions

The same settings and seed always give the same puzzle within a version of Simple Conveyor. A future version that improves the generator may change what a seed produces. Saved assets store the puzzle itself, not just its seed, so your saved puzzles and packs never change. Verified generation stores the seed that actually worked, such as level-7-5#1, so copying a puzzle's own seed always rebuilds that exact puzzle.

Console messages

  • "[Simple Conveyor] Documentation page not found": the documentation menu looks for these pages in a folder whose path contains SimpleConveyor and Documentation. Keep the Documentation folder inside the Simple Conveyor folder.
  • "[Simple Conveyor] Generation failed": something went wrong on the window's generation thread. Please send the message with your settings and seed.
  • "[Simple Conveyor] Kept ... in the build for the demo": the build step wrote the look material, which is expected after you switch render pipelines.
  • "[Simple Conveyor] No lit shader found for the active render pipeline": the pipeline asset has no default material, so the demo may draw pink in a build. Give the pipeline asset a default material.
  • "[Simple Conveyor] Exported 12 file(s) from ...": a pack's Export all finished.
  • "Simple Conveyor hub: no picture for ...": a mode card's picture couldn't be drawn. The card still works without it.

About Simple Conveyor

Simple Conveyor is a conveyor belt puzzle generator for Unity, made by Living Failure. It builds puzzles where belts carry a piece their way until it comes to rest on a stop tile, and the player has to bring it to rest on the goal, with ice, switches that turn belts, gems to collect, holes, and resting places off the best route for chests or keys. Then it solves each puzzle exactly, stamps it with its proven par and a 1 to 5 star difficulty, and stores it as a ScriptableObject or exports it as a picture. A complete 3D demo with running belts, built entirely in code, is included.

Version 1.0, made and tested with Unity 6000.6 in URP, HDRP and the Built-in pipeline.

Design principles

  • Solve exactly. The par, the number of best solutions and whether the piece can get stuck come from searching every position a puzzle can reach, in every switch state, not from an estimate.
  • Measure, then promise. The star bands, the presets, Auto's fixes and the demo's difficulty recipes all come from measuring real puzzles, hundreds at a time, and the docs say so where it matters.
  • Be honest. When a request can't be met, the puzzle's notes say so. Nothing is quietly downgraded, and the store page claims nothing the solver can't back up.
  • Belts that make sense. No belt points into a wall, a hole or off the board, whichever way the switches have turned it, and no ride goes round for ever.
  • Every gimmick matters. A switch or gem the best solution could do without isn't left on the board, and every hole is one a ride really ends in. Gimmicks that measured as doing nothing on belts were left out of the generator.
  • One set of rules. The generator, the solver and the play rules your game uses all move the piece the same way, so your game and the par always agree.
  • Named by what it does. The data says Arrow, Normal, Obstacle and Hazard, never belt, sand, crate or lava, so the same puzzle can be a factory floor, a mine cart track or a river of currents.
  • Not by colour alone. Every kind of tile has its own shape as well as its own colour, in the pictures and in the demo, so colour-blind players can play every puzzle.
  • No bloat. The core has no package dependencies, and the demo and the editor tools live in their own assemblies. The demo needs no prefabs, models, textures or audio files.
