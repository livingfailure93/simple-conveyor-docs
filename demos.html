The demo scene

Open Assets/SimpleConveyor/Scenes/SimpleConveyorDemo.unity and press Play. The scene holds a camera, a sun, and one object called Simple Conveyor Demo with the ConveyorDemo component and the four level packs assigned. Everything else, from the belts and the puck to the menus, is built in code when the scene starts, so there are no prefabs, models or audio files to look after, and the one texture (the belts' chevrons) is drawn in code too.

The demo reads the mouse, touch and keyboard through the Input System package. If nothing happens when you tap or press keys, set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both.

One component: ConveyorDemo

Add ConveyorDemo to any object in a scene with a camera and press Play. When it starts, it builds:

  • a Board child with ConveyorBoardView, which draws the board, the puck and every tile, and ConveyorGame, which plays them;
  • the HUD (ConveyorHud) and the hub (ConveyorHub), both in uGUI with Unity's built-in font;
  • ConveyorBeeper for the sounds and ConveyorEffects for the effects.

It also gives the camera a plain background colour and a 30 degree field of view, and adds a sun if the scene has no light.

FieldWhat it does
CameraThe camera that shows the board. Empty uses the main camera, and if there is none, one is made.
UiThroughCameraDraw the HUD and the hub through the camera instead of over the screen, so the camera's own picture includes them.
PitchHow steeply the camera looks down at the board to start with, in degrees (55 by default). Players can tilt it while they play.
HdrpLookHDRP only: give the demo the same look as on the other pipelines. Turn it off to use your own volumes and lights.
BeltsPack, GemsPack, SwitchesPack, IceAndHolesPackThe level pack for each mode. Any can be empty, and that mode's Level Pack tab then says how to fill it.

The flow

1

Welcome

The first screen says what Simple Conveyor is and where to make your own puzzles, with the publisher's contact at the bottom. Let's play goes on, and About on the mode screen brings it back.

2

Pick a mode

Four cards, each with a picture of a small puzzle of that mode, drawn by the asset's own exporter.

3

Endless or Level Pack

Endless lets you pick Belts only or With ice and a difficulty from 1 to 5 stars, then makes a new puzzle to that order on a background thread. Level Pack shows the mode's levels as numbered buttons: grey still to do, the mode's colour solved, and gold solved on par. Play starts the first level you haven't solved. The demo remembers your choices for each mode while it runs.

4

Play

A card at the top left shows what to do (take the gems, then reach the goal), how the belts and stop tiles work, the board and its gimmicks, its stars, your moves against par and the hints used, how things stand (gems taken, the switched belts as at the start or turned, falls), and a line for messages such as why a move was refused. A button bar has Undo, Restart, Hint, Solve and Menu.

5

Win

A green ring pops on round the puck, the puck hops twice, confetti falls, and after a short pause a panel shows your moves, the par and a rating, with Replay, Menu and Next (or press Enter). In Endless, Next makes a new puzzle to the same order, and in a pack it goes to the next level, then back to the pack after the last one. Menu or Esc returns to the mode cards at any time.

The four modes

ModeEndless puzzles
BeltsBelts and stop tiles on a board that grows with the stars: 5 x 5 at 1 star, 7 x 7 at 2, 8 x 8 at 3, 10 x 10 at 4 and 12 x 12 at 5.
Gems1 gem at 1 and 2 stars, 2 at 3 and 4, and 3 at 5, on smaller boards (4 x 4, 6 x 6, 6 x 6, 8 x 8 and 9 x 9), because gems lengthen a puzzle so much.
Switches1 switch at 1, 2 and 4 stars and 2 at 3 and 5, on boards from 5 x 5 to 11 x 11. They reverse the switched belts, and at 4 and 5 stars every other belts-only puzzle uses Rotate switches instead.
Ice and HolesAlways with ice: 1 hole at 1 star, 2 at 2 and 3, 3 at 4 and 4 at 5, on the Belts mode's board sizes.

With ice adds ice patches to the Belts, Gems and Switches modes on the same board sizes. The recipes are in ConveyorEndless, and each one goes through ConveyorPresets.Tune and verified generation with the stars as its target, so the puzzle you get rates the stars you picked. They were measured by making 8 puzzles for every mode, board and star level: all 35 recipes landed on exactly the stars asked every time, and the slowest puzzle took about 2.3 seconds.

The level packs

The demo ships with 42 levels in four packs in Assets/SimpleConveyor/Generated. Each was made by the generator from a fixed seed with the demo's own Endless recipes, landed exactly on the stars it was made for, keeps the belt rule, and was played through to par to check it. They are ordinary ConveyorPuzzlePack assets, so you can open them in the inspector, read their analysis, or export them as printable pictures.

PackLevelsWhat's in it
Belts12Three levels at each of 1, 2 and 3 stars, two at 4 and one at 5, from 5 x 5 to 12 x 12. Levels 3, 6, 9 and 11 have ice.
Gems10Two levels at each star from 1 to 5, with 1 gem up to 3. Levels 4, 6 and 10 have ice.
Switches10Two levels at each star from 1 to 5, with 1 or 2 switches. Level 4 has ice, and levels 8 and 10 have Rotate switches.
Ice and Holes10Two levels at each star from 1 to 5, with 1 hole up to 4.

Your own packs: make puzzles in the Conveyor Generator, choose Save > Pack (all), and drag the pack into a slot on the ConveyorDemo component.

Progress: each level's best number of moves is saved in PlayerPrefs under SimpleConveyor.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>, so it stays on that device. The win panel says "Level complete!", "New best" or "Your best is" accordingly, and a level Solve helped with isn't recorded ("Solve helped, so this one isn't saved").

Playing

The controls are listed at the bottom of the screen while you play, and in the Quick Start. A few details:

  • Moving: the arrow keys, WASD and swipes move the puck the way that looks closest on screen, so the controls stay right however the camera is turned. A tap beside the puck moves it toward the tap. A key pressed mid-ride waits for the ride to end. The puck glides at about 0.07 seconds a cell, turning wherever a belt turns it.
  • The board reacts: the belts run all the time, their chevrons moving the way each belt carries the puck. When the puck comes to rest on a switch, every belt with yellow corners turns round. A gem spins up and away as the puck rides over it, leaving its outline behind.
  • A fall: a ride that ends in a hole plays out, the puck drops in, and then it goes back to where it was before that move. A fall isn't counted as a move, and the card counts the falls.
  • A refused move nudges the puck toward that way, and the card says why in plain words: "That's the edge of the board." or "A wall is in the way." Reaching the goal with gems left says how many are left.
  • Undo puts the board back as it was before the last move (a gem back, the switched belts turned back) and moves the puck back the way it came. Restart pops the puck back in on its start.
  • Hint floats a purple arrow over the puck the way a best solution moves next. Solve (or F) plays the rest by itself, one best move at a time. The button says Stop meanwhile, and F or R stops it.
  • The view: the camera frames the board in the space the HUD leaves free, in landscape or portrait, and again whenever the window changes shape. Turn, tilt and zoom it with the keys or the mouse, and when zoomed in it follows the puck.
  • The rating on the win panel is 3 pips on par, 2 a little over (up to 2 moves, or a quarter of the par on longer puzzles) and 1 beyond that.

Sounds and effects

ConveyorBeeper makes every sound in code when the demo starts: a swish as a move starts and a soft thud as the puck stops (a few versions of each, so a run of moves doesn't sound robotic), a dull bump for a refused move, a soft low clack when a belt turns the puck (belts turn it often, so it stays quiet), a clunk and a rising whirr when a switch turns the belts, a sparkle for a gem, a falling plop for a fall, a ping for a hint, a rewind for undo and restart, a win arpeggio and a button blip. M mutes it. There is no music.

ConveyorEffects adds small effects made from a pool of tiny cubes: a puff behind the puck as it sets off and ahead of it as it stops or bumps, a small puff where a belt turns it, a magenta ring and burst round a switch, an amber and white burst from a gem, a ring round a hole the puck falls into, and confetti over the whole board for the win. Nothing is created or destroyed while you play, and because the effects use the demo's ordinary materials, they look the same in every render pipeline.

Render pipelines

The demo works in URP (recommended), HDRP and the Built-in pipeline without any changes. Its materials use the active pipeline's default lit shader, including the belts' running texture, and a build step keeps that shader in your builds, so the demo doesn't draw pink on a phone.

HDRP lights in physical units, sets the exposure on its own and takes its ambient light from a sky, so the demo would come out dark and tinted. With HdrpLook on, DemoHdrp adds a global volume with a fixed exposure, no tone mapping or bloom, and a gradient sky in the demo's colours, clears the camera to the demo's background colour, and sets the sun in lux. That code only compiles when the HDRP package is installed.

The shipped scene was checked in all three pipelines: every material on the pipeline's lit shader, nothing pink, the belts running, every level played to par, and the pictures within a couple of shades of each other.

Using the pieces yourself

The demo components work on their own too. The smallest setup is one ConveyorGame, which adds its ConveyorBoardView automatically:

using SimpleConveyor;
using SimpleConveyor.Demo;

var game = new GameObject("Conveyor").AddComponent<ConveyorGame>();
game.Begin(puzzle);                                    // build the board and start a game
game.View.FrameCamera(Camera.main, pitch: 50f);        // the camera is yours: frame the board once
game.Message += text => Debug.Log(text);               // a refused move or a fall, in the player's words
game.Won += g => Debug.Log($"Solved in {g.Moves} moves, par {g.Par}");
game.Slide(Dirs.East);                                 // move from your own code (keys, swipes and taps work too)
  • Input: ConveyorGame reads the keys, swipes and taps itself while it is enabled. To drive it yourself, turn off KeyboardInput, PointerInput or both. Your code can move either way, with Slide(dir), Push(screenDirection) and Tap(screenPoint), and call Undo(), Restart(), ShowHint() and StartAutoSolve(). Set PointerBlocked so a press over your own UI doesn't move the puck.
  • The camera is yours unless you turn on CameraControls, which lets the game frame the board and gives players the turn, tilt and zoom controls. FrameAreas keeps the board in the parts of the screen your UI leaves free.
  • Events: Moved after every change of position, Won once when it is solved, Stuck when a move leaves no way to the goal (a generated puzzle never does that), Message for things a player should read, Cue for every moment worth a sound, and Moment for the same moments with a position and colour for effects.
  • Falls: the game puts the position back after a fall. If your game wants a fall to cost more, listen for ConveyorCue.Fall on Cue and do what you like, such as calling Restart().
  • A HUD: add a ConveyorHud and set its Game, or read Moves, Par, Falls, Solved, HintsUsed and CanUndo into your own UI.
  • The look: the colours, materials and the belt texture are in DemoLook.cs, the meshes in ConveyorMeshes.cs and ConveyorBoardView.cs. CellTime sets how long the puck takes to cross a cell (0.07 seconds by default), SwitchTurnTime how long switched belts take to turn (0.35), and the board view's BeltSpeed and BeltsRun how the belts run.
  • Without the 3D view: keep a ConveyorPlay and use ConveyorRules directly, as in the Quick Start.
